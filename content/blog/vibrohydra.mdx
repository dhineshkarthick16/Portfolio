## The Problem: Blind Spots in Pressurized Pipelines

Underground and underwater pipelines carry water, hydrocarbons, and chemicals across thousands of kilometers. Keeping them safe is a high-stakes engineering challenge fraught with blind spots:

1. **Hidden Internal Damage:** You cannot visually inspect the inside of a buried ductile iron or welded steel pipe to verify if a sediment blockage or stress-crack is forming.
2. **The Excavation Dilemma:** Even when a mass balance monitor detects a pressure loss, locating the exact breach point traditionally requires excavating kilometers of terrain, costing millions and creating extensive environmental disruptions.
3. **The Disaster Data Scarcity:** Catastrophic pipeline failures are rare, which paradoxically cripples standard machine learning models—there is practically zero labeled field training data representing active pipe blowouts before they occur.

VibroHydra addresses this by turning the physical pipe wall into an acoustic transmission medium. By "listening" to the structural vibrations and acoustic waves generated by fluid boundary-layer interactions, VibroHydra continuously classifies pipeline health and pinpoints structural leaks to sub-meter accuracy without excavation.

## 1. Physically-Grounded Acoustic Synthesis Engine

Because real disaster datasets are sparse, VibroHydra incorporates a physics-based acoustic synthesis simulator modeling fluid dynamics, turbulence, Reynolds number, and acoustic wall resonance across four operational states:

- **`NORMAL_FLOW`:** Vortex shedding resonance centered between 40–60 Hz with harmonic decay and a low-amplitude Gaussian turbulence background.
- **`LEAK`:** Base flow combined with high-frequency acoustic shear emissions (200–800 Hz) caused by high-pressure escaping fluid jets, modeled with randomized defect severity and sigmoid onset curves.
- **`BLOCKAGE`:** Downshifted fundamental frequency (18–35 Hz) accompanied by low-frequency slugging and surging modulations (0.5–3.5 Hz) and localized pressure-wave reflections.
- **`NO_FLOW`:** Baseline ambient thermal sensor noise floor with zero hydrodynamic excitation.

---

## 2. Real-Time Feature Engineering (DSP)

Raw acoustic data is passed through high-pass DC rejection and zero-mean detrending before extracting 13 deterministic time-domain and frequency-domain features:

- **Time-Domain Dynamics:** Root Mean Square (RMS) energy, peak-to-peak amplitude, kurtosis (transient burst detection), skewness, and crest factor.
- **Spectral Energy Distribution:** Zero-crossing rates, spectral centroid (tracking high-frequency leak energy shifts), spectral bandwidth, and band power ratios across sub-bands.

---

## 3. Dual-Layer Machine Learning Classifier

VibroHydra employs a dual-tiered machine learning strategy to solve the data scarcity paradox:

1. **Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (Isolation Forest):** Trained exclusively on normal laminar/turbulent baseline conditions. When an unfamiliar vibration signature appears, the anomaly score spikes automatically without requiring prior failure labels.
2. **Supervised Multi-Class Classifier (Random Forest):** Accurately discriminates between localized blockages, leaks, and operational surges with zero data leakage across train/test folds.

## 4. Acoustic TDOA Defect Localization

Knowing *that* a pipe is leaking is insufficient—maintenance crews need to know *where* to dig. VibroHydra models two virtual acoustic sensors clamped to the pipe surface spaced at distance $L$:

```
Sensor Node A                                   Sensor Node B
   [S1] <----------------- Distance L -----------------> [S2]
     |                                                     |
     +--- Acoustic Wave (v_p) <--- [*LEAK*] ---> Acoustic Wave (v_p) ---+
```

When a leak occurs at an unknown distance $x$ from Sensor Node 1, acoustic stress waves propagate bidirectionally along the pipe wall at acoustic speed $v_p$ (approx. $1200\text{ m/s}$ to $1500\text{ m/s}$ in liquid-filled steel).

By running **Generalized Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform (GCC-PHAT)** between both sensor streams, the acoustic Time Difference of Arrival ($\Delta t$) is extracted from the cross-correlation peak:

$$x = \frac{L - v_p \cdot \Delta t}{2}$$

This mathematical localization isolates the defect location with sub-meter accuracy along a 10-meter pipe segment, directly visualized on an interactive 1D schematic.

## 5. Live Streamlit Operations Dashboard

The monitoring system provides an operations cockpit built in Streamlit:
- **Live Oscilloscope & Spectrogram:** Real-time visualization of raw acoustic signals and corresponding FFT frequency distribution.
- **Diagnostic Health Meter:** Real-time health index, alert level, and class probability distribution.
- **Geographic 1D Schematic:** Visual marker pinning the exact defect coordinate along the pipeline cross-section.
- **Event Audit Log:** Persistent log tracking anomaly timestamps, detected defect type, and confidence levels.
