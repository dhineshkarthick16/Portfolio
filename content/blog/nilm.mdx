## The Vision: Intelligence from a Single Clamp

Traditional energy auditing faces an expensive dilemma: either install dedicated smart plugs behind every single home appliance—costing hundreds of dollars and cluttering wall outlets—or settle for a dumb whole-home utility meter that provides only an aggregated monthly kilowatt-hour bill with zero appliance breakdown.

**Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring (NILM)** breaks this tradeoff. By clamping a single non-invasive sensor at the main electrical distribution panel, NILM analyzes the micro-transients, harmonic distortions, and phase angles of the aggregate electrical waveform to decompose individual loads—distinguishing when a refrigerator compressor cycles on versus when an induction motor spins up.

## Hardware Conditioning Circuit

The primary challenge in mains AC electrical sensing on modern 3.3V microcontrollers (like the ESP32) is that AC current naturally alternates symmetrically between positive and negative voltages. Feeding a negative voltage directly into an ESP32 ADC pin will clamp the internal ESD protection diodes and damage the silicon.

```
       Mains AC Phase Line
               |
         [ SCT-013 CT ] (Current Transformer Clamp)
               |
               v
       +-----------------------------------------------+
       |             Burden Resistor (R_b)             |
       |  Converts secondary induced mA to AC voltage  |
       +-----------------------------------------------+
               |
               v
       +-----------------------------------------------+
       |       LM358 Virtual Ground Bias (+1.65V)      |
       |  Op-amp buffer shifts AC sine to [0V - 3.3V]  |
       +-----------------------------------------------+
               |
               v
       +-----------------------------------------------+
       |             ESP32 ADC Input (GPIO 34)         |
       |          FreeRTOS DMA Continuous Sampling     |
       +-----------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. SCT-013 Current Sensing & Burden Resistor
The non-invasive **SCT-013** current transformer clamps around the isolated live conductor. Depending on the model (e.g., 100A/50mA), the secondary coil outputs a small alternating current proportional to primary line current. An onboard burden resistor $R_b$ translates this current into a measurable AC voltage drop.

### 2. LM358 Active DC Bias Offset (+1.65V)
To center the AC signal cleanly within the ESP32's $0\text{V} - 3.3\text{V}$ input range:
- A precision resistor divider establishes a stable $+1.65\text{V}$ midpoint.
- The **LM358 operational amplifier** is configured as a unity-gain voltage follower (buffer) to provide an ultra-low impedance virtual ground.
- The AC signal from the CT is AC-coupled via a high-grade capacitor, oscillating gracefully around $+1.65\text{V}$ ($0\text{V}$ to $+3.3\text{V}$ peak-to-peak).

### 3. 4N35 Optocoupler Zero-Crossing Detector
Apparent current alone cannot distinguish between resistive heaters and inductive motors; power factor ($\cos \theta$) is critical:
- A **4N35 optocoupler** is tied across the mains AC voltage through a high-voltage current-limiting resistor network.
- As the voltage sine wave crosses the zero-voltage threshold, the optocoupler fires an isolated digital pulse directly to an ESP32 hardware interrupt pin.
- This creates total galvanic isolation, protecting the microcontroller from high-voltage line transients while establishing the exact time zero ($t_0$) of the voltage wave.

## Dual-Core Firmware & Phase Alignment

The ESP32 firmware leverages its dual-core architecture running FreeRTOS:

```
[Mains Voltage] ---> 4N35 Zero Cross ---> GPIO Interrupt (Core 0)
                                                 |
[Current Signal] ---> LM358 Shift ---> ADC Sampling (Core 0)
                                                 |
                       FreeRTOS RingBuffer FIFO  |
                                                 v
                       [Core 1: DSP Engine & Metric Extraction]
                       - True RMS Current (I_RMS)
                       - Phase Delay: Delta_t = t_I_zero - t_V_zero
                       - Power Factor: cos(theta) = cos(2*pi*f*Delta_t)
                       - Real Power: P = V_RMS * I_RMS * PF
```

1. **Core 0 (Ultra-Fast Acquisition):** Executes high-rate ADC sampling (1000+ samples per $50\text{Hz}$ cycle) in a deterministic loop and captures the hardware timestamp of the 4N35 zero-crossing pulse.
2. **Core 1 (DSP & Power Factor Calculation):** Calculates the true root-mean-square current:
   $$I_{RMS} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} (I[i] - I_{offset})^2}$$
   By subtracting the zero-crossing timestamp of the current waveform from the voltage zero-crossing timestamp ($\Delta t = t_I - t_V$), the phase angle $\theta = 2\pi f \Delta t$ is extracted, yielding the true **Power Factor** $\text{PF} = \cos \theta$.
