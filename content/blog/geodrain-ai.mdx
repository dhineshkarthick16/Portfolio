## The Problem: Drainage Blindness in Rural India

In rural and semi-urban local administrative bodies (Gram Panchayats), monsoon rains frequently transform streets into stagnant pools, inundating agricultural land and triggering vector-borne epidemics. 

Despite the urgency, building effective storm drainage systems in rural areas remains crippled by systemic barriers:
1. **Costly Manual Surveying:** Commercial GIS contractors charge tens of thousands of rupees per square kilometer for topographic surveying, far exceeding rural Panchayat budgets.
2. **Intuition-Based Construction:** Without accurate slope contouring, local masonry drains are often dug haphazardly—sometimes resulting in drains sloping *uphill*, trapping stagnant wastewater.
3. **Data Disconnect:** While high-resolution satellite Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) are freely available globally, raw raster files cannot be read or utilized by local municipal officers.

**GeoDrain AI** bridges this gap. It provides an automated, browser-accessible decision-support system that converts satellite terrain rasters directly into optimized, gravity-assisted drainage network layouts, hydraulic specifications, and procurement-ready civil estimates.

## 1. Digital Terrain Model (DTM) Conditioning

Raw satellite elevation models frequently contain small sensor noise pits and digital artifacts where water appears trapped. GeoDrain AI ingests satellite elevation rasters and applies the **Planchon-Darboux sink-filling algorithm**:
- Artificial sinks and depressions are filled to their pour-point elevation.
- Continuous downward gradient is established across the terrain grid so simulated runoff flows uninterrupted toward natural drainage basins.

---

## 2. D8 Flow Direction & Accumulation Matrix

Using the conditioned elevation surface, the algorithm evaluates each cell relative to its 8 neighboring grid cells (the D8 model):
1. **Flow Direction Matrix:** Evaluates slope:
   $$S = \frac{\Delta z}{d}$$
   where $\Delta z$ is elevation difference and $d$ is horizontal distance ($1$ or $\sqrt{2}$). Water flows strictly toward the neighbor with the steepest negative descent.
2. **Flow Accumulation Grid:** By tracing all upstream tributary cells that drain through each point, high-accumulation channels naturally highlight historical stormwater pathways and flood-risk corridors.

---

## 3. Intelligent Alignment Optimization

Rather than designing arbitrary grid patterns, GeoDrain AI traces optimal pipe centerlines along natural dendritic flow paths:
- **Gravity-First Alignment:** Maximizes gravity flow to minimize or completely eliminate the need for costly electrical lift pumps.
- **Natural Outfall Connection:** Directs stormwater discharge directly into natural ponds, irrigation tanks (*eris*), or rivers, supporting local groundwater replenishment.
- **Excavation Depth Optimization:** Minimizes required trench cut-and-fill depths along roadsides to control civil construction costs.

---

## 4. Hydraulic Sizing via Manning's Equation

To translate geospatial vectors into civil construction specifications, the system calculates pipe and channel cross-sections using **Manning's open-channel equation**:

$$Q = \frac{1}{n} A R_h^{2/3} S^{1/2}$$

- $Q$: Peak stormwater runoff derived via the Rational Method ($Q = C \cdot I \cdot A$)
- $n$: Manning's roughness coefficient for precast concrete or masonry
- $A$: Wet cross-sectional area
- $R_h$: Hydraulic radius ($A / P$)
- $S$: Natural terrain slope gradient

## 5. Interactive Streamlit Operations Console

Built collaboratively by our team (**Keerthika N, Prithish S, Lakshmi Priya S, and Dhinesh Karthick R**), the web platform allows non-technical administrators to:
- Select village boundaries directly on an interactive satellite base map.
- View interactive 3D terrain slope heatmaps and water accumulation vectors.
- One-click generate comprehensive civil planning reports: total required pipe length (in meters), optimal pipe diameter brackets, excavation volumes ($\text{m}^3$), and estimated material budgets.
